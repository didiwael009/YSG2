## Should SaaS companies copy Hootsuite's homepage strategy? When it works and when it doesn't

### The pattern

Hootsuite's evolution shows what happens when a homepage gradually shifts from explaining what you do to proving you are the category authority. The change is not one rewrite — it is a series of small moves that all point in the same direction: toward buyers who already know what social media management software is and are now comparing vendors.

### Who should copy this

Copy this if your brand name already completes the sentence "We use [your company] for [category]." If a marketing manager at a Fortune 500 company can justify your product to procurement without first explaining the category, you have the recognition this positioning requires. You can afford to skip the explanation and lead with authority claims.

### Who should NOT copy this

Do not copy this if most of your homepage traffic arrives through non-branded search or paid ads where the visitor does not yet know your name. When a first-time visitor lands on formal capability language like **"the world's deepest social intelligence"** without understanding what the product does, they leave. The cost is qualified pipeline from cold traffic — the exact segment most growing SaaS companies depend on.

### The test before you copy

Open an incognito window and search the problem your product solves — not your brand name. Click your own paid or organic result. Does the headline make sense to someone who has never heard of you? If you need to scroll to understand what the product does, your homepage already skipped the explanation. That works only if your brand is doing that work before the page loads.
