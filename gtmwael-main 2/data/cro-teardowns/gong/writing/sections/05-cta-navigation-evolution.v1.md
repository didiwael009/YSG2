## How Gong removed 'What is RI?' from its nav and what that says about its sales motion

### How Gong's CTAs shifted from peer validation to ROI evidence

The original CTAs assumed a visitor who needed convincing by a familiar face. **"Paul Santarelli VP of Sales"**, **"Bevin Lyon VP, Customer Strategy"**, and **"Tonni Bennett VP of Sales"** were the calls to action — named practitioners vouching for the product to other practitioners.

The new CTAs assume a different visitor entirely. **"Read the case study"**, **"See Gong in action"**, **"Learn about our product"**, and **"View more customer stories"** point toward evidence and discovery rather than peer endorsement. This suggests the page is now built for someone evaluating a budget decision, not a sales rep looking for permission to champion the tool.

### What Gong's navigation removals say about who it no longer needs to educate

Eight navigation items were removed across this period. The pattern signals that Gong may no longer need to explain what its category is — the visitors arriving are already past that question.

Eight items were also added, including **"Pricing"** and **"Book a demo"**. Both are bottom-of-funnel actions — the kind a visitor takes when they have already decided to evaluate, not explore. Adding them to the primary nav compresses the sales motion: fewer steps between landing and committing to a conversation.

The removal of feature-specific education and the addition of direct conversion paths together suggest the homepage is now built to accelerate buyers who arrive informed, not orient buyers who arrive curious.

**Founder test:** Pull up your own nav and count how many items exist to explain what your product is versus how many exist to move a ready buyer forward. If explanation items outnumber conversion items, your nav may be optimized for the wrong visitor.
