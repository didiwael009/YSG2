## How Gong's CTA and navigation changes signal a shift in its sales motion

### How Gong's CTAs shifted from peer endorsement to product evidence

The original CTAs put real people front and centre. **"Paul Santarelli VP of Sales"**, **"Bevin Lyon VP, Customer Strategy"**, and **"Tonni Bennett VP of Sales"** were the actions a visitor could take — named practitioners vouching for the product to other practitioners.

The new CTAs point toward evidence instead. **"Read the case study"**, **"See Gong in action"**, **"Learn about our product"**, **"Visit the solutions hub"**, and **"View more customer stories"** invite the visitor to explore proof rather than connect with a peer. This suggests — if the swap was intentional — that the page is now built for someone already evaluating options, not a sales rep looking for internal permission to champion the tool.

### What Gong's navigation changes say about how it moves buyers forward

Eight navigation items were removed and eight were added across this period. The pattern suggests Gong may no longer need to explain what its category is — visitors arriving may already be past that question.

The additions point toward bottom-of-funnel actions — steps a buyer takes only when they are ready to evaluate, not just explore. If that shift was deliberate, it suggests the nav is now structured to move informed buyers forward rather than orient visitors who are still figuring out what the product does.

If your nav still leads with education items — pages that explain what your product is or who it is for — you may be slowing down the buyers who are already ready to talk.

**Founder test:** Count the items in your own nav. How many exist to explain your product? How many exist to move a ready buyer toward a conversation? If explanation items outnumber the rest, your nav may be optimised for the wrong visitor.
