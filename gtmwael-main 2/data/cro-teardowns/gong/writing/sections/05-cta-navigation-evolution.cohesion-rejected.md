## How Gong removed 'What is RI?' from its nav and what that says about its sales motion

### Gong's CTA changes: from named peer validation to destination-focused actions

The same informed-buyer logic reshaped the page's buttons and links. Gong removed named VP testimonials as clickable CTAs. If your homepage still uses a named person — **"Paul Santarelli VP of Sales"**, **"Bevin Lyon VP, Customer Strategy"**, **"Tonni Bennett VP of Sales"** — as a button or link, check whether that person is driving clicks or just occupying space.

The removed CTAs, including **"Watch Video"**, were built for someone still deciding whether to trust the product. What replaced them — **"Read the case study"**, **"See Gong in action"**, **"Visit the solutions hub"**, **"View more customer stories"**, and **"Learn about our product"** — are destination-focused actions aimed at someone already comparing options rather than deciding whether to believe the product works. The page now offers fewer low-commitment entry points and more decision-oriented CTAs, consistent with a page built for visitors who arrive already informed.

### Gong's navigation changes: what eight added items signal about the sales motion

**"Pricing"** and **"Book a demo"** were added to Gong's navigation. If your nav contains neither, ask whether your current visitors have a clear next step once they decide they are ready to move. Those two items are the most direct signal of where the page now wants visitors to go.

Eight nav items were removed — including **"What is RI?"**, which explained the Revenue Intelligence category (the idea that sales teams should use data from customer calls and deals to improve their performance). Also removed: **"Coaching Recommendations"** and **"Deal Visibility"**, two feature-level pages built for someone still exploring what the product does. The eight items added include destination pages like **"Pricing"** and **"Book a demo"** alongside broader hubs. Together, the additions suggest a nav rebuilt for visitors who arrive knowing what Gong is — and need directions to a decision, not an explanation.
