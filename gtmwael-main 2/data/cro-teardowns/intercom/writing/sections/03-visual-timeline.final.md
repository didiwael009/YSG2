## From "support customers" to "the only helpdesk for AI" — a category ownership bet

Intercom's homepage now asks you to believe a new era has arrived, and they own it.

In January 2023, the H1 read **"Support customers at exactly the right moment"** — a product benefit anyone could claim. By October 2025, it shifted to **"Intercom is the AI customer service company"** — identity-first positioning. The current H1 goes further: **"The only helpdesk designed for the AI Agent era."** That's not a feature. It's a market definition with Intercom at the center.

This framing embeds a bold assumption: that "the AI Agent era" is real, recognized, and imminent enough that buyers are already shopping for it. If true, Intercom owns the search. If premature, the claim lands as noise. The supporting sections reinforce the bet — **"A complete solution for AI and human agents"** and **"A fully-featured AI-powered helpdesk"** build a coherent category story rather than listing capabilities.

The tradeoff: outcome promise vs category claim. The old headline promised a result. The new one demands belief in a paradigm.

**So what?** Before claiming a category, ask: is your buyer already searching for this frame, or are you teaching them a vocabulary they didn't ask for?
