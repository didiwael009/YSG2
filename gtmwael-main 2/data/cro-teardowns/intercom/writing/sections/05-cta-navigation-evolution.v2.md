Intercom's CTA set shifted from outcome-driven action phrases to a product-taxonomy model. The old CTAs—"Start growing business non-stop", "Start converting customers faster", "Start using next-generation support today"—promised a business result in plain language. The new set introduces "Fin AI Agent", "Fully-featured helpdesk", "Natively integrated AI Agent", and "AI-powered Insights" alongside "Contact sales" and "Start free trial". That architecture can be read as a bifurcated funnel: self-serve trial for smaller buyers, sales motion for enterprise accounts evaluating multiple platform components.

The trust burden differs sharply across these paths. "Start growing business non-stop" required a visitor to believe one click would begin momentum—friction is low, proof burden is deferred to the post-click experience. "Fin AI Agent" as a CTA assumes the visitor already knows what Fin is, what an AI agent does in Intercom's architecture, and which workflow it unlocks. That label works when the visitor is mid-evaluation and comparing feature matrices, but adds cognitive load for a support leader scanning the page to understand if Intercom solves their workflow. Product-label CTAs likely serve mid-market buyers comparing platforms; outcome CTAs served early-stage founders looking for a fast-start tool.

Navigation taxonomy tightened: five items were added, eight removed. The removed labels included broader discovery paths ("See all stories", "Watch a Demo", "Read story"). The added set is not fully detailed in the evidence, but the CTA evolution suggests the new structure likely mirrors product categories rather than buyer jobs. One founder takeaway: if you replace action CTAs with product-name CTAs, the surrounding H2s and proof elements must pre-load enough context that a cold visitor can predict what clicking "Fin AI Agent" will deliver. Without that scaffolding, differentiation becomes friction.
