The old CTA set included outcome-oriented phrases: "Start growing business non-stop", "Start converting customers faster", "Start using next-generation support today". The new set replaces most of these with "Contact sales", "Start free trial", and product component labels like "Fin AI Agent" and "Fully-featured helpdesk". That shift can be read as a bifurcated funnel: one path for self-serve trial qualification, one for sales contact. The friction direction is mixed—trial reduces friction for lower-tier buyers, while product-label CTAs assume the visitor already understands what "Fin AI Agent" delivers before clicking.

Navigation changes are harder to isolate from the evidence provided, but the CTA label evolution from action verbs to product taxonomy suggests the page now expects buyers to arrive with category awareness. A visitor who lands on "Natively integrated AI Agent" or "AI-powered Insights" must first map their need to Intercom's architecture. That works when the visitor is comparing platforms in a formal evaluation, but adds load for a support leader who arrived looking for "better ticket resolution" and now must decode which component solves that job.

One founder takeaway: CTA labels are positioning claims. Changing "Get started" to "Fin AI Agent" trades immediate clarity for product differentiation, but only if the surrounding copy has already built enough context that the visitor knows what clicking that button will unlock. If your page uses product names as CTAs, audit whether a cold visitor can predict the next screen.
