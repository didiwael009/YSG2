## The Risk of Claiming a Category Before You Own It

Category claims, compressed CTAs, executive-tier language—the pattern is seductive. But it carries risk.

Do not copy this homepage. Not until your product can survive a category-defining claim under scrutiny. Intercom's evolution represents a deliberate compression: from broad platform to singular AI bet, from feature menu to belief statement, from multiple buyer paths to one confident ask. It looks like clarity.

But the final homepage works because Intercom already has the proof infrastructure to support **"The only helpdesk designed for the AI Agent era."** They have Fin as a named, demonstrable product. They have the brand recognition to make a superlative stick. They have the sales team to catch enterprise buyers who need hand-holding after a bold claim. Most early-stage companies have none of these.

The tradeoff: category claim in exchange for a higher proof burden and narrower buyer access. If your product cannot immediately validate the promise, the mismatch creates friction at every downstream step—trial, onboarding, renewal.

**So what?** Before borrowing this playbook, run one test: can a first-time visitor verify your headline claim within their first product session? If the answer is no, the ambitious homepage becomes a conversion liability, not an asset.
