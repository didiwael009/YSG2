## Should SaaS companies copy Stripe's homepage strategy? When it works and when it doesn't

### The pattern

Stripe moved from explaining features to asserting category ownership. The headline stopped describing what Stripe does and started claiming what Stripe is: the infrastructure layer itself. This is maturity-driven positioning — when the brand has enough recognition that the page can skip the "what is this" stage and speak only to buyers who already know.

### Who should copy this

If most of your homepage traffic arrives already knowing what your product does — through brand search, direct traffic, or word-of-mouth — you have the recognition this requires. The test: open an incognito window and search your company name. If the search results page already explains your category before anyone clicks, you may be able to remove that explanation from the homepage itself.

### Who should NOT copy this

If you still depend on non-branded search, paid ads to cold audiences, or referrals from content that does not explain what you do, removing the category explanation will cost you qualified pipeline. Visitors will bounce because they cannot figure out what you sell. Aspirational language without category context reads as vague, not authoritative.

### The test before you copy

Check your homepage traffic sources for the last 30 days. If more than 40% is branded search or direct, and your bounce rate on non-branded traffic is already high, test a headline that assumes prior knowledge. If non-branded traffic converts, keep the category explanation.
