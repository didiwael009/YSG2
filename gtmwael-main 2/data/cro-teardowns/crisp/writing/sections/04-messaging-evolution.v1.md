## How Crisp's messaging shifted its audience

### Who the old page served

The old page spoke to small-business owners looking for an affordable way to manage customer conversations. The page title called itself the **"#1 Business Messaging Platform For Startups & SMB's"** and the meta description promised a **"14 Days free trial. No credit-card required. Try now!"** This is vocabulary for a buyer who needs to be convinced to start — not someone already evaluating platforms.

### Who the new page serves

The new page assumes the visitor already understands what a customer support platform does. The headline now reads **"Augment your customer experience. AI made to support your team & customers."** The page title calls Crisp **"The AI Customer Support Platform for Every Business"** — no startup qualifier, no trial urgency. This reads like positioning for a buyer who is comparing vendors, not discovering the category.

### What this means for the sales process

The page no longer tries to close a self-serve signup on the spot. It expects visitors who will explore the platform, request a demo, or talk to sales before committing. The shift is subtle but directional: less friction to start the trial, more friction to understand whether Crisp fits. Does your homepage still assume the visitor needs convincing — or are you writing for someone who already knows what they want?
