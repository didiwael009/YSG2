## Should SaaS companies copy Crisp's homepage strategy? When the narrowing approach works and when it doesn't

### The pattern

Between July 2022 and June 2026, Crisp added **6 new section headings** and removed **3**, while adding **8 navigation items** without removing any. The homepage expanded in scope while the headline narrowed in audience. This suggests the company shifted educational content away from the hero section and into deeper pages, allowing the top of the page to filter rather than explain.

### Who should copy this

If your homepage receives mostly branded search traffic — visitors who already searched for your company name — you can follow Crisp's model. If your product category (the type of software, like "customer messaging platform" or "project management tool") is already clear from your domain name or from content visitors read before landing on your homepage, the page can skip the explanation stage.

### Who should NOT copy this

If your homepage gets non-branded search traffic, cold paid ads, or referrals from general business directories, copying Crisp's approach may cost pipeline. Visitors who land without knowing what your product does will leave or misunderstand your positioning. Crisp added navigation depth to carry the educational load — if you narrow the headline without adding that structure, you remove context without replacing it.

### The test before you copy

Open your analytics. Filter homepage visits by traffic source. If more than half come from non-branded channels, your page likely still needs category explanation in the hero section. Then check your navigation: Crisp added **8 items** to support visitors who needed more context. If you have fewer than five navigation options and most traffic is cold, you cannot afford to narrow yet.
