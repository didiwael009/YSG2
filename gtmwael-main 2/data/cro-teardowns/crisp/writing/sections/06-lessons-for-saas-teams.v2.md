## Should SaaS companies copy Crisp's homepage strategy? When it works and when it doesn't

### The pattern

Crisp added 8 navigation items between Jul 2022 and Jun 2026 while removing zero — the opposite of what most conversion advice recommends. At the same time, the page added 6 new H2 section headings and removed only 3. This suggests the company shifted from simplifying the homepage to expanding what it covers, likely because the visitor arriving at the page changed.

### Who should copy this

If a stranger can type your product name into Google and land on five comparison articles before they reach your homepage, you may have the brand recognition this requires. The test is not traffic volume — it is whether your visitors arrive already knowing what live chat software does. Crisp can afford to add navigation complexity because the page no longer has to explain the tool type itself.

### Who should NOT copy this

If your homepage still receives visitors from non-branded search terms like "customer support chat tool" or cold paid ads, adding navigation items without clear labels may fragment attention before the visitor understands what you sell. The risk is not aesthetic — it is that the page assumes understanding the visitor does not yet have.

### The test before you copy

Count how many of your current homepage H2 section headings explain what the product does versus what it enables. Crisp removed 3 H2s and added 6 — check whether your current headings still define the tool category, or whether they assume the visitor already knows. If more than half are explanatory, you likely still need them.
