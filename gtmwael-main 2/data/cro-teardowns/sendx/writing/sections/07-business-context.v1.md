## Why SendX stopped competing on features and started competing on inbox placement

SendX launched in 2018 into the most crowded segment of martech: affordable email marketing. The category had two types of tool — expensive feature-complete platforms (HubSpot, Pardot) and affordable challenger tools (Mailchimp, ActiveCampaign, Drip) — and the challenger space was already full. SendX's early positioning was the standard challenger playbook: feature parity at a lower price, easier to use than the legacy tools. This worked as a differentiation strategy in 2018–2021 when price sensitivity was the dominant buying criterion for SMBs.

Between 2021 and 2025, the category dynamics changed. Mailchimp was acquired by Intuit in 2021 and moved upmarket, frustrating many SMBs. Klaviyo went public in 2023 and dominated ecommerce. Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) rebranded and claimed the SMB CRM category. In this environment, being "affordable and feature-rich" stopped differentiating — every sub-$50/month tool made the same claim. The insight that appears to have driven SendX's homepage rewrite is that **deliverability** is the most painful unsolved problem for users of lower-cost email tools. When you pay less, you share infrastructure with thousands of other senders — and shared infrastructure means your emails land in spam when your neighbors abuse it. SendX's positioning appears to claim that its infrastructure is the exception.

The "Ready to make email work again?" section heading is the strategic bet made visible: SendX is targeting competitor migrations, not first-time buyers. The meta description proof point — "95–98% inbox placement across 4.4 billion emails" — is designed to be checkable by a prospect who A/B tests deliverability across tools. For SaaS teams in commoditized categories, this is the clearest model of what "double down on your one defensible advantage" looks like as a homepage strategy.
