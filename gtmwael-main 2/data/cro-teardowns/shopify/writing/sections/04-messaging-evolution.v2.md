## Shopify stopped naming the category and started naming the person

The old headline — **"The platform commerce is built on"** — spoke to a merchant comparing tools. The new one — **"Be the nextAI all-star"** — speaks to someone deciding whether they can build at all.

The meta description changed from **"Try Shopify free and start a business or grow an existing one"** to **"Try Shopify free. Build or grow your business fast with AI."** The punctuation shift is minor. The headline shift is not. **"The platform commerce is built on"** positions Shopify as the category leader for established sellers. **"Be the nextAI all-star"** positions the reader as the protagonist — it speaks to first-time founders or pre-revenue side-hustlers who need identity framing before they evaluate features. This can be read as targeting aspiring solopreneurs rather than experienced operators shopping on capability.

The tradeoff: **speed vs qualification**. Aspirational language lowers entry friction for browsers still deciding whether to sell online, but it may pull in readers who haven't qualified themselves as serious buyers. That increases trial volume but risks diluting conversion if the page attracts explorers rather than starters.

The question for your own page: are you naming the category (**"The platform commerce is built on"**) or naming the person (**"Be the nextAI all-star"**) — and does that framing match the buyer who actually signs up and stays?
