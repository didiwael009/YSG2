## What SaaS teams can study from Unbounce's homepage positioning overhaul

### Category keyword removal — Unbounce dropped "landing page builder" from both H1 and meta description

Unbounce removed its core category keyword from the headline and meta description entirely. The old meta read **"Try the best landing page builder."** The new headline is simply **"Launch faster. Convert more."** No category label, no channel anchor — the brand name does the explaining now.

This pattern is worth testing only if your brand already ranks on page one for your category keyword without a paid ad. If it does not, removing that keyword from the H1 may lower your paid search Quality Score — the relevance rating Google uses to set your cost-per-click — and raise what you pay per visitor.

Search your core category keyword right now. If your brand appears organically on page one, the keyword may be removable. If it does not, keep it in the H1 until it does.

---

### Product navigation expansion — Unbounce added A/B Testing, Smart Traffic, and AI Copywriting as standalone nav items

Unbounce's old navigation listed **"Preview the Builder · Features · Templates."** The current version promotes **"A/B testing · Smart Traffic · AI Copywriting"** as separate nav nodes alongside vertical segments like Agencies, Ecommerce, and SaaS. Each node signals a distinct product, not a buried feature.

This approach makes sense if customers already use those products together in real workflows. Add a nav node for a product your customers rarely use alongside the core tool, and the page suggests breadth the product experience may not yet deliver.

Check your product analytics: do at least 20% of active accounts use two or more of those products in the same month? If not, hold the nav expansion until usage catches up.

---

### CTA hierarchy — Unbounce expanded from three CTAs to six with no clear visual dominant

Unbounce's original page ran three CTAs: **"PREVIEW THE LANDING PAGE BUILDER · SEE HOW UNBOUNCE CAN HELP ME · SEE THE PLANS."** The current page runs six, including **"Start A/B testing · Learn the basics · Explore our resources · Get support."** Six paths at equal visual weight may leave visitors without an obvious next step.

According to the *Landing Page Playbook* benchmark data from Wael Aouididi, conversion rates tend to fall when pages offer more than two equally weighted CTAs. One entry point should dominate visually; secondary paths should sit clearly below it in size and colour.

Screenshot your homepage and count every button. If more than two share the same size and colour, you have no dominant path. Demote the secondary ones before running any other test.
