## How Unbounce's H1 traded a specific ROI claim for a platform promise — and who that reveals as the new buyer

### The headline shift: from a channel-specific outcome to a speed-and-conversion promise

The 2019 H1 was **"Convert More Leads and Sales From Your Ad Spend"** — a direct ROI claim aimed at someone managing a paid media budget. The current H1 is **"Launch faster. Convert more."** Every channel-specific word is gone. This suggests the page is no longer written for the PPC manager justifying a tool purchase, and is instead aimed at a broader growth team evaluating a full workflow.

### The meta description shift: from tool swap to platform growth

The 2019 meta opened with **"Ditch your other landing page software"** — a direct call to switch from a competitor. The current version reads **"Grow your leads and sales with Unbounce."** Gone is the switcher framing. Gone too are the ad-spend signals that helped paid search visitors confirm they had landed in the right place. The page title shifted similarly: from **"Landing Page Builder & Conversion Platform for Marketers | Landing Page Software"** to **"The Best Landing Page Builder and CRO platform | Unbounce"** — dropping the repeated "software" anchor in favour of the broader CRO category.

### What the messaging shift signals about the new target buyer

The page now reads like it is written for a growth team or agency evaluating an integrated build-test-optimise workflow — not a performance marketer looking for a faster way to cut cost-per-lead. That is a larger addressable market. But it may come at a cost: removing **"ad spend"** and **"cost-per-lead"** from the headline and meta description reduces the message-match between the page and anyone arriving from a high-intent paid search query. Visitors who clicked an ad for "landing page builder for PPC" may not immediately recognise the page as solving their specific problem.

The practical test: visit your own homepage and count how many measurable outcomes appear above the fold — numbers, percentages, channel names. If the answer is zero, your page may be making the same trade Unbounce made: broader appeal, less immediate relevance to the buyer who already knows exactly what they need.
