## How Unbounce replaced 'SEE THE PLANS' with product-specific entry points — and what that did to the sales motion

### How Unbounce's CTAs shifted from two high-commitment paths to ten specific entry points

The broader buyer also reshaped where the page asks visitors to click. The evidence shows Unbounce removed six CTAs and added ten across the period studied. The original set pointed toward one decision: **"SEE THE PLANS"** and **"PREVIEW THE LANDING PAGE BUILDER"** both assumed a visitor close to committing. So did **"SHOW ME HOW"** and **"SEE HOW UNBOUNCE CAN HELP ME"** — each pushing toward a single next step.

The replacements assume a wider range of visitors. **"Start A/B testing"**, **"Learn about Concierge plans"**, and **"Learn the basics"** each address a different intent level — from a new explorer to an enterprise evaluator. **"Request a demo"** and **"Get support"** suggest the page may now be serving buyers who want assisted sales alongside those ready to self-serve.

That shift from six CTAs to ten distinct entry points signals the page may no longer be routing every visitor toward one path. If your homepage does the same, check whether a visitor ready to buy can identify their path in under five seconds — or whether the options compete for equal attention and slow them down.

### What the removed CTAs signal about Unbounce's changing sales motion

The removals are as telling as the additions. **"SHOW ME HOW"** and **"SEE HOW UNBOUNCE CAN HELP ME"** are discovery-oriented — they speak to someone who does not yet know what the product does. Removing them suggests the page may no longer be written for that visitor.

What replaced them — **"Request a demo"** and **"Get support"** — points toward buyers who already understand the category and want either a guided sale or an existing-customer resource.
