## What SaaS teams can study from Unbounce's decision to erase its own category label

### The earned erasure rule — Unbounce dropped "landing page builder" from H1 and meta only after a decade of brand equity

Unbounce's meta description once read **"Ditch your other landing page software. Try the best landing page builder."** The current version never says "landing page builder" at all. That phrase — the exact category keyword — simply disappeared. The new headline is **"Launch faster. Convert more."** No category, no channel, no explanation.

This matters because the category keyword is doing double duty. It tells Google what your page is about, which protects your paid search Quality Score — a measure of how well your ad and landing page match what someone searched for. Erase the keyword before your brand name carries that weight, and your ad costs may rise.

Run this test now: open Google and search your core category keyword (e.g. "landing page builder"). Check whether your brand appears on page one without a paid ad. If it does not, your homepage still needs that keyword in the H1. Do not erase it yet.

### The acquisition-to-navigation test — Unbounce added A/B Testing and Smart Traffic as standalone nav nodes

Unbounce's old navigation listed **"Preview the Builder · Features · Templates."** The new version includes **"A/B testing · Smart Traffic · AI Copywriting"** as discrete product nodes — each with its own nav entry. That is a signal to visitors that these are real, separate products, not buried features.

The risk: navigation signals a promise the product experience must then keep. This is consistent with Moore's whole-product model in *Crossing the Chasm* — buyers evaluate whether the full solution exists, not just the headline feature. Add a nav node for a product your customers rarely use together, and the page overpromises.

Check your own analytics: do at least 20% of your active accounts use both products in the same month? If not, the nav node may signal breadth your product cannot yet deliver. Hold it until usage catches up.

### The dual-funnel CTA structure — Unbounce now runs six CTAs where it once ran three

Unbounce's old CTAs were **"PREVIEW THE LANDING PAGE BUILDER · SEE HOW UNBOUNCE CAN HELP ME · SEE THE PLANS."** The new page lists six: **"Start building for free · Start A/B testing · Learn about Concierge plans · Learn the basics · Explore our resources · Get support."** Six parallel paths signal uncertainty about who the primary buyer is.

The Landing Page Playbook benchmark data from Wael Aouididi shows conversion rates drop when pages offer more than two equally weighted CTAs — visitors stall when no single next step is obvious. One free-start CTA should dominate visually; an enterprise path should sit clearly below it.

Audit your homepage CTA hierarchy in under five minutes: screenshot the page and count every button. If more than two share the same size and colour, you have no dominant path. Pick one winner and demote the rest visually before running any other test.
