## Unbounce homepage 2019–2025: from gradient outcome hero to polished platform showcase

### 2019 — a single tool, a single promise

Behind that headline swap sits a six-year visual arc. The 2019 homepage was built around one idea: ad spend should convert. The headline said exactly that: **"Convert More Leads and Sales From Your Ad Spend"**. The layout was a centered hero with a blue gradient background, two outlined CTA buttons below the headline, and no social proof above the fold. The evidence describes the visual sophistication as a 3 out of 5 — a developing brand still explaining what it does.

A section heading deeper on the page made the pitch more explicit: **"See How You Can Lower Cost-Per-Lead By Over 70% With Landing Pages"**. This was a page built to justify the product category, not to assume the reader already understood it.

### 2022 — the first visible confidence shift

By October 2022, the centered gradient was gone. In its place: a dark charcoal hero, a yellow illustration of a person alongside a marketing interface, and a left-aligned white headline. That is a meaningful visual shift — from "explain and invite" to "show and assert."

The layout moved to full-bleed. The visual sophistication score rose to 4 out of 5, described in the evidence as a developing brand with noticeably more design maturity. The page started showing the product, not just describing it.

### 2024–present — platform layout, polished execution

The current page carries a visual sophistication score of 4 out of 5 — now described as polished rather than developing. The hero shows a product screenshot with interface mockups and side annotation graphics. A five-icon feature row sits below the centered tagline **"Launch faster. Convert more."**

A new section heading, **"Convert more customers with one platform"**, signals a wider scope than landing pages alone. The page looks less like a single tool and more like a suite.

**Test:** Visit your own homepage and count how many distinct product capabilities are visible above the fold. If the answer is one, your layout may be underselling what the product has become.
