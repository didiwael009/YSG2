## How Unbounce's H1 traded a specific ROI claim for a platform promise — and who that reveals as the new buyer

### Unbounce headline shift: from ad-spend ROI to speed-and-conversion promise

The visual maturity tracked a parallel shift in the words. In 2019, Unbounce led with **"Convert More Leads and Sales From Your Ad Spend"** — language aimed squarely at someone managing a paid media budget. The current H1 is **"Launch faster. Convert more."** Every channel-specific word is gone. This suggests the page is now written for a broader growth team evaluating a multi-product workflow, rather than a performance marketer justifying a single tool purchase.

### Unbounce meta description and section heading shift: from competitor swap to platform growth

The 2019 meta opened with **"Ditch your other landing page software"** — a direct call to switch. The current version reads **"Grow your leads and sales with Unbounce. Easily create, test, and optimize landing pages, and boost conversions using AI insights."** The switcher framing is gone. So is the old section heading **"See How You Can Lower Cost-Per-Lead By Over 70% With Landing Pages"** — replaced by **"Convert more customers with one platform."** That swap removes a specific, provable metric and replaces it with a capability claim.

### What Unbounce's messaging shift may signal about message-match risk

The page no longer leads with ad-spend language or a quantified cost-per-lead claim. If paid search drives a significant share of Unbounce traffic on high-intent queries like "landing page builder," visitors scanning for that specific ROI signal may not find it quickly — and that recognition gap may slow conversion for that audience.

The page is now more consistent with targeting growth teams evaluating an integrated set of tools: landing pages, A/B testing, and AI optimisation together.

### Quick test: count the outcome signals in your first 50 words of body copy

On your own homepage, count the words that name a channel — paid, organic, social — or a specific metric — cost-per-lead, ROAS, conversion rate — in the first 50 words of body copy. If the count is zero, you have made the same trade Unbounce made: broader appeal, but fewer instant confirmation signals for the visitor who arrives knowing exactly what problem they need to solve.
