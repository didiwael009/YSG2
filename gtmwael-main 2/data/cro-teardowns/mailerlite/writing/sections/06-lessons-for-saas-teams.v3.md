## What SaaS teams can study from MailerLite's homepage evolution

### Name your buyer in the nav, not just your product — MailerLite added "Solopreneurs" and "Small business" as segments

MailerLite added **"Solopreneurs"** and **"Small business"** to its navigation alongside product categories like Newsletters and Websites. This is not a feature page — it is an audience segment that lets the visitor self-identify immediately. The page becomes relevant faster when the buyer sees their own description in the nav.

This pattern is worth testing when your product serves two or more distinct buyer types who arrive with different questions. A solopreneur building a paid newsletter and a team email marketer need to see different things on the same page. A segment nav item routes them without making them read all the copy.

Visit your navigation today. If every item is a product or feature name, try adding one buyer-type item ("For freelancers," "For teams," "For agencies") and measure whether time-on-page or scroll depth increases for that segment.

### Pre-empt the complexity objection with a named section — MailerLite added "Keeping it Lite" as platform expanded

As MailerLite's H1 expanded to list five products, the page added a section heading: **"Keeping it Lite."** This is an on-page objection response. It names the tension — "you are adding features, does this get harder to use?" — and answers it in the page structure, not in fine print.

This applies when your product is adding features that might worry your existing buyers. A user who chose you for simplicity will feel the homepage no longer speaks to them if it now lists five products with no acknowledgment that the product stayed approachable. A named section costs nothing to test.

Write one H2 that explicitly names your core promise in the context of your expansion: "Still the fastest setup in [category]" or "All the features, none of the learning curve." Test whether bounce rate changes for returning visitors.

### Dual CTA for two buyer intents — "Sign up free" + "Watch demo" on the same page

MailerLite's primary CTA section offers **"Sign up free"** (self-serve, immediate) and **"Watch demo"** (assisted, evaluative) side by side. This is not a fallback — it is a deliberate two-path design. The individual creator signs up now. The team buyer watches a demo first.

This pattern is relevant when your product has crossed from pure self-serve into team or business purchases. A single "Start free trial" CTA optimizes for the individual. Adding a demo path captures buyers who need to evaluate before committing, without removing the direct signup for those who are ready.

Run a simple test: add a secondary "Watch demo" CTA below your primary one. Track whether the demo-path segment has a higher average contract value or shorter time-to-paid than the free trial segment. That data will tell you whether to invest in the demo path or drop it.
