## How Agorapulse's Homepage Messaging Shifted from Simplicity to ROI — and Who It Is Now Selling To

### Agorapulse's headline shift: from ease-of-use to business outcomes

The old headline promised relief: **"Social Media Management Simplified."** The current one makes a commercial case: **"Drive Real Impact on Social Media. From Engagement to ROI."** Dropping "Simplified" and introducing "ROI" suggests the page is now written for someone who has to justify the software spend to a manager — not someone just looking for an easier tool.

### Agorapulse's page title and meta description: removing the price and effort signals

The page title moved from **"Simple & Affordable Social Media Management | Agorapulse"** to **"Social Media Management Software | Agorapulse."** Two words — "Simple" and "Affordable" — gone. That removal may indicate a deliberate choice to stop attracting cost-sensitive buyers shopping on price. The meta description followed: the old version ended with a free-trial push; the new one reads as a neutral category statement, listing features without urgency.

### What Agorapulse's messaging shift signals about its target buyer

Agorapulse replaced community-identity headings with scale proof and outcome headings across the page. **"Who loves Agorapulse?"** and **"We are a people company"** became **"Over 31K+ social media managers use our tool daily"** and **"Effective social media management with impactful business outcomes."** That is a shift from warmth to evidence — from how the product feels to what it can demonstrate.

That shift is consistent with a page now written for marketing professionals who report upward — people who need to show their work pays off. Notably, the outcome language in the H1 and the outcome language in the section headings point in the same direction, which suggests a full-page realignment rather than a one-off headline experiment. ROI language in a headline is only credible if the rest of the page can support it — and here, the section headings do exactly that.

**Founder test:** Visit your homepage and count how many words describe how the product feels to use versus how many describe a result your buyer can report to their boss. If the feeling words outnumber the result words, your messaging may still be written for the wrong room.
