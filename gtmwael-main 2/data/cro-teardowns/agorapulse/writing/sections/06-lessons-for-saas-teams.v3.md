## What SaaS teams can study from Agorapulse's decision to erase 'Simple & Affordable' from its homepage

### Only claim outcomes your product can already deliver — Agorapulse's H1 shifted to ROI language alongside deeper outcome messaging across the page

Agorapulse's H1 moved from **"Social Media Management Simplified"** to **"Drive Real Impact on Social Media. From Engagement to ROI."** — and section headings shifted in the same direction, including **"Effective social media management with impactful business outcomes"**. The messaging change was consistent across the page, not isolated to the headline.

That consistency suggests the shift followed product capability rather than leading it. If your product cannot surface a specific ROI metric inside the trial experience, outcome language in the headline may cost credibility with exactly the buyer it is trying to reach.

Visit your homepage and read the H1 aloud. Then open your product and ask: does a specific feature deliver this outcome today, without a workaround? If the answer is no, consider holding the outcome language until the product catches up.

### Removing review-site CTAs — Agorapulse cut G2, TrustRadius, and GetApp links and replaced them with direct trial and demo paths

Agorapulse removed **"Read on TrustRadius"**, **"Read on G2 Crowd"**, and **"Read on GetApp"** entirely, replacing them with **"Try for free now"** and **"Watch an On-Demand demo"**. That is a bet that the brand no longer needs third-party platforms to earn a click.

Hormozi notes in *$100M Offers* that a strong offer reduces the buyer's need to look elsewhere before deciding. That principle may apply here — but only if buyers are already arriving with brand awareness, not discovering Agorapulse for the first time through a search.

Open Google and type your brand name followed by "vs". If autocomplete surfaces named competitors, buyers are already comparing you by name and this pattern may be worth testing. If nothing appears, keep the review-site links.

### Removing affordability signals to reach a different buyer — Agorapulse dropped 'Simple & Affordable' from its page title

The page title changed from **"Simple & Affordable Social Media Management | Agorapulse"** to **"Social Media Management Software | Agorapulse"**. Three words gone — and with them, the primary signal to buyers who evaluate tools on price and ease.

That removal signals a shift toward buyers who justify software spend to stakeholders, not buyers who compare monthly fees. If your closed-won data skews toward team leads, simplicity and affordability words are likely filtering in visitors you cannot convert anyway.

Open your current page title tag. Count words that signal price, ease, or simplicity. Then pull your last ten closed-won deals and check whether the decision-maker was a team lead or an individual contributor. Let that data guide whether those words stay.
