## How Agorapulse's H1 retired 'Simplified' and replaced it with 'ROI' — and what that says about who it is selling to now

### Agorapulse's headline shift: from ease-of-use to business outcomes

The old headline promised relief: **"Social Media Management Simplified."** The current one makes a commercial case: **"Drive Real Impact on Social Media. From Engagement to ROI."** That is not a cosmetic update. Dropping "Simplified" and introducing "ROI" suggests the page is now written for someone who has to justify the software spend to a manager — not someone just looking for an easier tool.

### Agorapulse's page title and meta description: removing the price and effort signals

The page title moved from **"Simple & Affordable Social Media Management | Agorapulse"** to **"Social Media Management Software | Agorapulse."** Two words — "Simple" and "Affordable" — gone. That removal may indicate a deliberate choice to stop attracting cost-sensitive buyers who are shopping on price. The meta description followed the same direction: the old version ended with an exclamation mark and a free-trial push; the new one reads as a neutral category statement, listing features without urgency.

### What Agorapulse's messaging shift signals about its target buyer

The section headings complete the picture. **"Who loves Agorapulse?"** and **"We are a people company"** — warm, community-oriented language — were replaced with **"Over 31K+ social media managers use our tool daily"** and **"Effective social media management with impactful business outcomes."** Scale proof and outcome language replaced identity and sentiment. Together, these shifts are consistent with a page now written for marketing professionals who report upward — people who need to show their work pays off, not just people who want their work to feel easier.

The March 2022 launch of Agorapulse's Social Media ROI feature likely gave the product the evidence to support this language. You cannot credibly claim ROI in the headline if the product cannot measure it.

**Founder test:** Visit your homepage and count how many words are about how the product feels to use versus how many describe a result your buyer can report to their boss. If the feeling words outnumber the result words, your messaging may still be written for the wrong room.
