## What SaaS teams can study from Agorapulse's decision to erase 'Simple & Affordable' from its homepage

### Letting product features earn messaging permission — Agorapulse waited for a real ROI dashboard before leading with ROI

Agorapulse changed its H1 from **"Social Media Management Simplified"** to **"Drive Real Impact on Social Media. From Engagement to ROI."** The word ROI did not appear in the headline until the product could actually produce ROI reporting. The page title shifted from **"Simple & Affordable Social Media Management | Agorapulse"** to **"Social Media Management Software | Agorapulse"** at the same time.

This matters because buyers who are mid-evaluation — comparing two or three tools seriously — will check whether the product backs up the claim. Outcome language that outruns the product loses credibility with exactly the buyer it is trying to reach.

Visit your own homepage and read the headline. Then open your product and ask: does a feature exist that delivers this specific outcome? If the answer requires a workaround, the headline is ahead of the product. Fix the product first, or walk the headline back.

### Removing review-site CTAs — Agorapulse cut G2 and TrustRadius links and replaced them with direct trial and demo paths

Agorapulse removed CTAs labeled **"Read on TrustRadius"**, **"Read on G2 Crowd"**, and **"Read on GetApp"** entirely. In their place: **"Try for free now"** and **"Watch an On-Demand demo"**. This only works if buyers are already searching for Agorapulse by name — not discovering it through review platforms.

Hormozi notes in *$100M Offers* that the strongest offer removes the buyer's need to shop elsewhere. But that only holds when the buyer has already decided the category is right. Removing comparison-site links before your brand earns that recognition may simply send visitors away with nowhere to go.

Run this diagnostic: open Google and type your brand name followed by "vs" — do autocomplete suggestions appear? If yes, buyers are already comparing you by name and your brand may be strong enough to own the evaluation. If no results appear, keep the review-site links.

### Stripping affordability signals as an ICP declaration — Agorapulse chose one buyer type by removing three words from its page title

Dropping **"Simple & Affordable"** from the page title is not a price change. Agorapulse's pricing page did not move. What moved was which buyer the homepage optimises for: the team lead who justifies software spend upward, not the solo manager comparing monthly fees.

This is an ICP declaration — ICP stands for ideal customer profile, meaning the specific type of buyer you build the page for. Every signal you remove narrows that audience. Removing affordability language does not repel everyone, but it stops attracting the buyer for whom price is the deciding factor.

Open your current homepage title tag. Count how many words describe price, ease, or simplicity. Then ask: is your sales team consistently closing team leads or individual contributors? If team leads dominate your closed-won deals, those words may be attracting the wrong visitor volume before they even click through.
