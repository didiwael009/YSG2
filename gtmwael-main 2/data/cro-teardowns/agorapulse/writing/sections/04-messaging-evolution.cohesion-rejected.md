## How Agorapulse's H1 retired 'Simplified' and replaced it with 'ROI' — and what that says about who it is selling to now

The palette signaled a new buyer; the words confirmed it.

### Agorapulse's headline shift: from ease-of-use to business outcomes

The old headline promised relief: **"Social Media Management Simplified."** The current one makes a commercial case: **"Drive Real Impact on Social Media. From Engagement to ROI."** Dropping "Simplified" and introducing "ROI" suggests the page may now be written for someone who needs to show their work pays off — not someone just looking for an easier tool.

### Agorapulse's page title and meta description: removing the price and effort signals

The page title moved from **"Simple & Affordable Social Media Management | Agorapulse"** to **"Social Media Management Software | Agorapulse."** Two words — "Simple" and "Affordable" — gone. That removal may signal a de-emphasis of price-sensitivity as a primary draw. The meta description followed: the old version ended with a free-trial push; the new one reads: **"An easy to use Social Media Management Software that allows you to stay organized, save time, and easily manage your inbox, publishing, reporting, monitoring, and team collaboration tools."** Urgency and exclamatory language are out. Category statement is in.

### What Agorapulse's messaging shift signals about its target buyer

Agorapulse replaced community-identity headings with scale proof and outcome headings across the page. **"Who loves Agorapulse?"** and **"We are a people company"** became **"Over 31K+ social media managers use our tool daily"** and **"Effective social media management with impactful business outcomes."** That is a shift from warmth to evidence — from how the product feels to what it can demonstrate.

The outcome language in the H1 may now resonate most with marketing professionals who report upward — people who need results they can put in front of a manager, not just a tool that makes their day easier.

The H1 and the section headings point in the same direction. **"Effective social media management with impactful business outcomes"** reinforces the same ROI frame that the headline opens with — consistent with a page-wide shift, not a single headline swap, if the rest of the evidence holds.

---

**Founder test:** Visit your homepage and count how many words describe how the product feels to use versus how many describe a result your buyer can report to their boss. If feeling words outnumber result words, your messaging may still be written for the wrong room.
