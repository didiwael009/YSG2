## How Agorapulse removed TrustRadius and G2 from its homepage to own the evaluation process itself

### CTA changes: what Agorapulse stopped sending visitors away to read

The removed CTAs assumed visitors needed external proof before deciding. **"Read on TrustRadius"**, **"Read on G2 Crowd"**, **"Read on GetApp"**, and **"Reviews on G2CROWD"** all sent visitors to comparison grids where Hootsuite and Sprout Social appear alongside Agorapulse.

The new CTAs assume the visitor is ready to evaluate Agorapulse directly. **"Try for free now"**, **"Watch an On-Demand demo"**, **"Book a demo"**, and **"Sign up!"** move visitors toward a decision on the page itself. This suggests the homepage no longer treats third-party review sites as a necessary stop — if the visitor's intent on arrival supports skipping that step.

### Navigation changes: how Agorapulse's homepage nav shifted from explanation to action

The core finding lands here first: Agorapulse's navigation moved away from passive category labels and toward conversion actions built into the top bar itself. That shift signals the homepage may no longer assume visitors need orienting before they act.

What was removed supports this. **"See all pricing"** and **"Start my free trial now"** functioned as transactional exits — links that send visitors away from the page before they convert — and both are gone.

**"Learn more"** and **"Read more"** were also removed. Their absence suggests the page expects visitors to arrive already knowing enough to take a next step rather than browse.

**"Sign up!"** was added as a primary navigation action — a conversion step in the top bar, visible before a visitor reads a single line of copy.

**Founder test:** Open your own homepage navigation. If your primary nav items are still category labels like "Features" or "Solutions," your navigation is explaining your product rather than moving visitors toward a decision. Count how many nav items link to an action versus a category page.
