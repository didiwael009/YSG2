## How Agorapulse removed TrustRadius and G2 from its homepage to own the evaluation process itself

### CTA changes: from third-party validation to direct trial

The old CTAs handed visitors off. **"Read on TrustRadius"**, **"Read on G2 Crowd"**, **"Read on GetApp"**, and **"Reviews on G2CROWD"** all assumed the visitor needed external proof before deciding — and sent them to grids where Hootsuite and Sprout Social appear alongside Agorapulse.

The new CTAs pull visitors inward instead. **"Try for free now"**, **"Watch an On-Demand demo"**, **"Book a demo"**, and **"Sign up!"** assume the visitor is ready to evaluate Agorapulse directly. This shift suggests the homepage no longer treats third-party comparison sites as a necessary step in the buying process.

### Navigation changes: what Agorapulse stopped needing to explain

The removed navigation items pointed outward or explained basics: **"See all pricing"** and **"Start my free trial now"** functioned as transactional exits. What replaced them signals a different expectation of the arriving visitor.

**"Sign up!"** and **"See a demo"** are now primary navigation actions — conversion steps built into the top bar, not buried below the fold. **"Agorapulse AI New!"** appears as a named navigation item, which indicates the company wants AI visible before a visitor reads a single paragraph.

**"Learn more"** and **"Read more"** were removed entirely. Their absence suggests the homepage no longer assumes visitors need orienting — it assumes they arrive with enough context to act.

**Founder test:** Open your own homepage navigation. If your primary nav items are still category labels like "Features" or "Solutions," your navigation is explaining your product rather than moving visitors toward a decision. Count how many nav items link to an action versus a page.
