## What SaaS teams can study from Agorapulse's decision to erase 'Simple & Affordable' from its homepage

### Only claim outcomes your product can already deliver — Agorapulse's H1 shifted to ROI language alongside deeper outcome messaging across the page

Don't lead with outcome language if your product cannot yet demonstrate it. Agorapulse's H1 moved from **"Social Media Management Simplified"** to **"Drive Real Impact on Social Media. From Engagement to ROI."** — and that shift was accompanied by section headings like **"Effective social media management with impactful business outcomes"**, suggesting the messaging followed capability rather than leading it.

This matters if your buyers are mid-evaluation — comparing two or three tools seriously — because outcome language that outpaces what the product can show may cost credibility with exactly the buyer it is trying to reach.

Visit your homepage and read the headline aloud. Then open your product and ask: does a specific feature deliver this outcome today, without a workaround? If not, consider walking the headline back until the product catches up.

### Removing review-site CTAs — Agorapulse cut G2, TrustRadius, and GetApp links and replaced them with direct trial and demo paths

The practical lesson here: pulling external review links in favour of **"Try for free now"** and **"Watch an On-Demand demo"** is a bet that your brand no longer needs third-party validation to earn a click. Agorapulse removed CTAs labeled **"Read on TrustRadius"**, **"Read on G2 Crowd"**, and **"Read on GetApp"** entirely.

Hormozi notes in *$100M Offers* that a strong offer reduces the buyer's need to look elsewhere before deciding. That principle may apply here — but only if buyers are already arriving with brand awareness, not discovering Agorapulse for the first time through a search.

Open Google and type your brand name followed by "vs". If autocomplete surfaces named competitors, buyers are already comparing you by name and this pattern may be worth testing. If nothing appears, keep the review-site links.

---

### Removing affordability signals to filter for a different buyer type — Agorapulse dropped three words from its page title and changed who the homepage speaks to

The finding is direct: dropping **"Simple & Affordable"** from the page title signals a shift toward buyers who justify software spend to stakeholders, not buyers who compare monthly fees. This is an ICP shift — ICP means ideal customer profile, the specific type of buyer the page is built to convert. Agorapulse's pricing page did not change; the signal did.

Every word you remove from a headline or title narrows your audience. Removing affordability language may stop attracting cost-sensitive visitors — but it may also stop reassuring them if they arrive anyway.

Open your current page title tag. Count words that signal price, ease, or simplicity. Then check your closed-won deals: do team leads or individual contributors dominate? If team leads dominate, those words may be filtering in the wrong visitor.
