## How Expensya removed 'Free trial' and rebuilt its funnel around the French-speaking CFO

### What the CTA shift says about who Expensya now expects to arrive

The original CTAs assumed a visitor who was still exploring. **"Free trial"**, **"Discover our solutions"**, **"Find out more"**, and **"Discover all integrations"** are all low-commitment, self-serve actions — they let the visitor move at their own pace.

The new primary CTA is **"Réservez une démo"** — French for "Book a demo." That is not an invitation to explore. It is a handoff to a sales conversation.

Removing **"Free trial"** is the sharpest signal here. Self-serve access — where a visitor signs up and tests the product without talking to anyone — appears to have been deprioritised entirely. The page now assumes the visitor is ready to speak to someone, not ready to click around alone.

### What the navigation changes signal about size-based segmentation

The evidence shows 8 navigation items removed and 8 added — a complete rebuild, not a light edit.

The additions include a language toggle, **"Anglais"** — English. That is a deliberate choice worth noting. English capability is preserved, but subordinated: the page defaults to French, and English becomes an option rather than the primary experience. This suggests the homepage is now optimised for a French-speaking buyer first.

What is absent from the new navigation is any company-size tier. Previously, visitors could self-select by company stage. That mechanism is gone. Size-based segmentation likely still happens — but now inside the sales conversation rather than on the homepage.

**Founder test:** Open your own navigation. If visitors can currently identify which tier they belong to without talking to you — ask whether you want that decision made before or after a sales conversation.
