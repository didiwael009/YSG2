## How Expensya's H1 shift from English automation to French aspiration redefined its target buyer

### The headline stopped describing a product and started addressing a buyer

The old headline said **"Automated spend management for all companies"** — a functional claim, in English, aimed at nobody in particular. The new headline says **"Voici à quoi devrait ressembler la gestion des dépenses."** — "Here is what expense management should look like" — in French. That is not a copy tweak. Writing the headline in French signals that the page is no longer trying to speak to every market equally. It suggests the primary visitor Expensya now wants to attract already thinks in French.

### The meta description shifted from process steps to emotional contrast

The old meta description read: **"From paperless invoices to reimbursement, Expensya software and mobile app automate every step of the spend management and expense reporting process."** That is a checklist of features. The new one reads: **"Remplacez le stress et les frictions liés à la gestion des dépenses et des notes de frais des employés par la clarté et la simplicité : voici Expensya, le logiciel intelligent de gestion des dépenses pour les équipes financières modernes."** The shift is from "here is what the product does" to "here is how you currently feel — and here is the alternative." It names **"les équipes financières modernes"** — modern finance teams — directly, which may indicate the page is now written for a finance director or CFO rather than a general operations buyer.

### What the messaging shift signals about who Expensya is now written for

The page title also moved from **"Automated spend management for all companies"** to **"Gestion des dépenses des employés et cartes de paiement | Expensya"** — naming payment cards and employee expenses specifically, not a broad platform. Together, the headline, meta description, and title changes are consistent with a page now built for one type of reader: a French-speaking finance professional who is accountable for compliance and spend control, not an IT buyer comparing integrations.

The section headings reinforce this. Where the old page offered **"Integration with your infrastructure and ecosystem"**, the current page leads with **"Cartes de paiement & budgets"**, **"Politiques de dépenses"**, and **"Multi-devises et multi-pays"** — payment cards, expense policies, multi-currency. Those are compliance and control pillars, not breadth claims.

**Founder test:** Visit your homepage and count how many words describe what your product does versus how many describe who your buyer is and what they are responsible for. If the ratio is heavily weighted toward features, your page may be explaining rather than qualifying.
