## What SaaS teams can study from Expensya's market-retreat homepage strategy

### Language as market commitment — Expensya switched its entire homepage to French

Expensya replaced **"Automated spend management for all companies"** with **"Voici à quoi devrait ressembler la gestion des dépenses"** — a full language switch, not a translation toggle. The navigation retained an **"Anglais"** toggle, preserving English access without rebuilding the page. The primary frame is now French, but the door stays open.

This pattern is worth testing if your analytics already show one language-market generating the majority of your revenue. Switching primary language before that signal exists may collapse traffic from other regions without a replacement pipeline ready to absorb the loss.

Open your analytics right now and find which country or language drives your top 20% of revenue. If it is not already the language your homepage speaks, you have a gap worth investigating before your next homepage refresh.

### Compliance framing as a CFO unlock — Expensya added "Politiques de dépenses" and "Améliorez la conformité"

Expensya removed **"Expensya fully automates your business expenditure"** and added section headings like **"Politiques de dépenses"** (Expense policies) and **"Améliorez la conformité. Réduisez les risques."** (Improve compliance. Reduce risk.) Automation breadth disappeared. Compliance specificity replaced it.

This shift matters when regulatory pressure gives a finance leader a reason to act now. Compliance language elevates the conversation from operations to finance leadership — the person with budget authority — which may shorten sales cycles if your product genuinely addresses a compliance requirement.

Visit your homepage and count how many section headings name a risk or a regulatory outcome versus a feature or workflow. If the answer is zero, test one compliance-framed heading in your most finance-relevant section and measure scroll depth and demo requests against the current version.

---

### Keeping the language toggle while removing the trial — Expensya preserved re-expansion optionality

Expensya dropped **"Free trial"** entirely and replaced it with **"Réservez une démo"** and **"Contacter un commercial"**. But the navigation still shows **"Anglais"** — a language toggle that keeps the English version one click away. The trial is gone. The international exit is not.

This is a safer architecture than deleting English access entirely. If French-market growth stalls, the homepage can re-expand without a full rebuild. Removing a trial CTA without a functioning sales team ready to convert demo requests may cause a sharp drop in leads with no offsetting pipeline to replace it.

Check your current homepage: do you have a way to re-enter a previous market or buyer segment without rebuilding from scratch? If not, consider preserving at least one low-friction path — a toggle, a secondary CTA, a language redirect — before committing to a full narrowing.
