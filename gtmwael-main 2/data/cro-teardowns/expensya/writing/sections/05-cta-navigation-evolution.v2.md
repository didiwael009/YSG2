## How Expensya removed 'Free trial' and rebuilt its funnel around the French-speaking CFO

### What Expensya's CTA shift says about the visitor it now expects

The original CTAs assumed a visitor who was still browsing. **"Free trial"**, **"Discover our solutions"**, **"Find out more"**, and **"Discover all integrations"** all let the visitor move at their own pace — no sales conversation required.

The new primary CTA is **"Réservez une démo"** — French for "Book a demo." That is not an invitation to explore. It is a direct handoff to a sales conversation.

Removing **"Free trial"** is the clearest signal. The page no longer appears to offer a way in without talking to someone first. It suggests Expensya now expects a visitor who is ready to commit time, not just click around.

### What Expensya's navigation rebuild signals about its intended audience

Eight navigation items were removed and eight added — a complete rebuild, not a light edit.

The removed items included no language preserved in the new navigation. Every retained or added item in the evidence is in French. This is consistent with the homepage now being built primarily for a French-speaking buyer.

What the new navigation does not appear to contain is any mechanism for visitors to sort themselves before a conversation. Size-based segmentation — letting visitors pick their company size to see relevant content — is absent from the listed nav items. That decision may now happen inside the sales conversation instead.

**Founder test:** Look at your own CTAs. Count how many let a visitor try or explore your product without speaking to anyone — the way Expensya's old **"Free trial"** and **"Discover our solutions"** did. Then ask: does that match the sales motion you actually want? If your intended next step is a demo call, your CTAs should say so — the way **"Réservez une démo"** now does.
