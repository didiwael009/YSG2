## How Buffer's CTAs and navigation changed between 2019 and 2026 — and what the shift signals about its growth model

### How Buffer's CTAs changed: from one destination to five

The original CTA — **"Get Started for Free →"** — assumed the visitor had already decided what they wanted. One button, one path, one product.

The new homepage lists five parallel calls to action: **"Learn more about Publish"**, **"Learn more about Create"**, **"Learn more about Community"**, **"Learn more about Analyze"**, and **"Learn more about Collaborate"**. That shift suggests Buffer no longer expects visitors to arrive knowing which product fits their job.

The structure mirrors a freemium model where each product has its own entry point. A visitor picks the track that matches what they need, rather than committing to a single undifferentiated product upfront.

### How Buffer's navigation changed: fewer pages for buyers who are comparing options

Eight navigation items were removed across the period tracked. The evidence does not name each item individually, but the pattern is visible in what disappeared from the CTA layer: **"See All Case Studies →"** was removed entirely.

That removal signals social proof as a conversion tool — the kind aimed at visitors at the top of the funnel (people just starting to look, not yet ready to buy) — was deprioritized. The homepage appears to shift toward product exploration rather than third-party validation.

Eight navigation items were also added. The direction, combined with the CTA expansion, suggests the page is now built to route visitors to a specific product, not to convince a single undecided buyer.

**Founder test:** Count the CTAs on your own homepage. If there is one, ask who it is written for — a visitor who already knows your product, or one who is still figuring out which tool they need. If most visitors arrive cold, a single CTA may be filtering out more buyers than it converts.
