## How Buffer replaced its single conversion funnel with a five-product discovery menu — and what that says about its new growth model

### How Buffer's CTAs changed: from one destination to five

The original CTA — **"Get Started for Free →"** — assumed the visitor had already decided Buffer was the product they wanted. One click, one path.

The new homepage lists five parallel calls to action: **"Learn more about Publish"**, **"Learn more about Create"**, **"Learn more about Community"**, **"Learn more about Analyze"**, and **"Learn more about Collaborate"**. That shift suggests Buffer no longer expects visitors to arrive knowing which product they need.

This structure only works because each product has its own free entry point. There is nothing to negotiate. A visitor picks the track that matches their job, enters free, and upgrades if they need more. The CTA menu is the pricing model made visible.

### How Buffer's navigation changed: removing the evaluation infrastructure

The navigation lost **"Pricing"** and **"Case Studies"** — two pages that exist specifically for visitors who are actively comparing options before buying.

Their removal suggests Buffer is no longer building its homepage around that comparison moment. Instead, channel-specific paths now appear in the nav, capturing visitors who arrive searching for Instagram or TikTok tools — people at the top of the funnel, not the bottom.

**"See All Case Studies →"** also disappeared from the CTA layer. That is the clearest signal: social proof as a conversion tool has been deprioritized in favor of product exploration.

**Founder test:** Open your own navigation. If **"Pricing"** is still a top-level nav item, ask whether your visitors are arriving to compare — or to explore. If most come in cold through paid or organic search, a product-first nav may convert better than an evaluation-first one.
