## Buffer homepage 2019–2025: from scattered-dot startup hero to polished multi-product workspace

### 2019 — a broad welcome for anyone managing social media

The 2019 homepage opened with a centered layout: headline centered, CTA centered, social proof logos arranged in a horizontal row below. The visual sophistication score sat at 2 out of 5 — described in the evidence as early-stage.

The headline was explicit and wide: **"Save time managing social media for your business."** Sections below named pricing directly — **"Find the plan that's right for you"** and **"Buffer for Business Pricing"** — signaling a page built to move evaluators toward a decision.

Scattered colored dots appeared as decorative accents. They would survive every redesign that followed.

---

### 2024 — the layout breaks open, the headline breaks down to one word

By July 2024, the layout had shifted from centered to multi-column. A product screenshot carousel appeared on the right side of the hero. A statistics row landed below the fold. Proof density moved from low to medium.

The headline changed to a single word: **"Buffer."**

New section headings appeared — **"Grow your audience on social and beyond"** and **"Build a following without draining your time"** — framing the page around aspiration rather than features. The visual sophistication score reached 3 out of 5, described as developing.

---

### 2025–present — polished layout, darker top bar, same colored dots

The current page carries a visual sophistication score of 4 out of 5 — described in the evidence as polished. The layout is full-bleed. A dark announcement bar now sits at the very top of the page.

The headline remains **"Buffer."** A statistics row and product screenshot stay in the hero. The colored dot accents — present since 2019 — are still there, now described as a refined geometric accent system.

CTAs like **"Get Started for Free →"** are gone. The page no longer pushes toward a single action.

**Test it on your own site:** count how many calls to action appear above the fold, then ask whether each one points to the same next step or pulls visitors in different directions.
