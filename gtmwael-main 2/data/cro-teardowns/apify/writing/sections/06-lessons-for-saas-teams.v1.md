## What SaaS teams can study from Apify's decision to let its community write its headline

### Ecosystem count as H1 — Apify replaced its core promise with **"39,044 tools for your AI"**

Apify's old H1 was **"Extract data from any website"** — a utility promise. The new one leads with a specific tool count and names AI agents as the beneficiary. The number does the work: it signals a platform, not a product, and implies competitors cannot replicate the supply quickly.

This pattern is worth testing only if your ecosystem is large, verifiable, and still growing. A specific number that reads as authority at 39,000 reads as fragility at 300. If visitors can sense the count is padded or stagnant, the specificity hurts more than a vaguer claim would.

Visit your homepage and swap your H1 for your largest defensible ecosystem number — integrations, templates, community scripts. Ask three people outside your company whether the number feels like a moat or a confession. That reaction tells you whether the pattern fits.

---

### Category reframing — Apify added a section heading that says **"Not just a web scraping API"**

That heading directly names Apify's old identity and then distances from it. It works here because returning visitors already know what Apify does — the reframe lands as evolution, not confusion. For cold traffic arriving without that context, the same line may just create doubt.

This is a high-risk move if your homepage still depends on non-branded search. Cold visitors need to know what you are before they can appreciate what you are becoming. Testing this with returning visitors first — via a targeted email or retargeting campaign — reduces the risk of losing new arrivals before they orient.

Go to your homepage analytics and check your new-versus-returning visitor split. If returning visitors are under 30% of your traffic, exposing this reframe to cold traffic without a controlled test may cost you more than it gains.

---

### Dual CTA structure — Apify runs **"Get started"** and **"Contact sales"** in the same hero

The hero now carries both a self-serve entry point and an enterprise track. That structure is coherent only if the product naturally routes different buyers into different experiences at signup. If both buttons lead to the same onboarding flow, the enterprise signal is decoration, not function.

Adding a **"Contact sales"** CTA signals enterprise intent to visitors — but it also raises their expectations. Buyers who click it expect a different conversation than a developer starting a free trial. If your sales team is not resourced to handle that volume separately, the second button may create a worse experience than none.

Open your own hero and trace both CTA paths to their destination. If they land on the same page or trigger the same email sequence, remove the secondary CTA until the two journeys are genuinely distinct.
