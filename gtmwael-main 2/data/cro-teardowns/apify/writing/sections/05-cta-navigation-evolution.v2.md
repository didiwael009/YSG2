## How Apify's CTA and navigation changes reveal a two-track sales motion

### How Apify's CTA changes split one funnel into two

The original **"Browse all actors"** assumed a visitor still exploring — someone who needed to see the catalogue before committing to anything. The new CTAs assume no such hesitation.

**"Get started"** points at a developer ready to build. **"Contact sales"** and **"Get a demo"** point at a buyer who answers to a budget committee. **"Read more customer stories"** points at someone building an internal case for sign-off.

Three different visitors, three different next steps — all served from the same homepage. This suggests Apify is now running two parallel funnels rather than directing everyone toward a single self-serve entry point.

---

### What Apify's navigation changes signal about its new target visitor

Apify's navigation now includes **"Find your Actor"** and **"Browse Apify Store"** — and dropped **"Browse all actors"**. That shift from open browsing to directed search signals a visitor who arrives knowing what they want, not one still learning what the product does.

Four navigation items were removed entirely. Eight were added. That volume of change suggests the old nav was built for a different kind of visitor than the one Apify now wants to reach.

**"New MCP connectors are live"** — added as a CTA, not a nav item — references Model Context Protocol, a technical layer that lets AI agents call external tools. Only someone actively building with AI agents would recognize that term. If Apify intends to narrow its audience, placing that language prominently may signal exactly who the page is now written for.

**Founder test:** Look at your own nav. If it still says something like **"Browse all actors"** — open-ended, catalogue-first — compare it against Apify's current **"Find your Actor"**. One invites browsing. The other assumes the visitor already has a job to do. If your visitors arrive informed, your nav should reflect that.
