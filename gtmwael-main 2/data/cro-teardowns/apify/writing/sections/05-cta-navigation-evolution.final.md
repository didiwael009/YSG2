## How Apify's navigation shift from 'Free store' to 'MCP Give your AI access to Actors' redefined its sales motion

### How Apify's CTA changes split one funnel into two

Those two conversations show up in the buttons too. The original **"Browse all actors"** assumed a visitor still exploring — someone who needed to see the catalogue before committing to anything. The new CTAs assume no such hesitation.

**"Get started"** points at a developer ready to build. **"Contact sales"** and **"Get a demo"** point at a buyer who answers to a budget committee. **"Read more customer stories"** points at someone building an internal case for sign-off.

Three different visitors, three different next steps — all served from the same homepage. If these CTAs are visible simultaneously above the fold, that is consistent with Apify running two parallel funnels rather than directing everyone toward a single self-serve entry point.

### What Apify's navigation changes signal about its new target visitor

Eight navigation items were added. Four were removed. That volume of change signals a significant shift in who the nav is built to serve — not a minor refresh.

Among the CTAs removed was **"Browse all actors"** — open-ended, catalogue-first. Among the CTAs and nav items added: **"Find your Actor"** and **"Browse Apify Store"**. That shift from open browsing to directed search suggests a visitor who arrives knowing what they want.

**"New MCP connectors are live"** — added as a CTA — references Model Context Protocol, a standard that lets AI agents call external tools. Only someone actively building with AI agents would recognise that term. Placing that language prominently may signal exactly who the page is now written for.

**Founder test:** Look at your own navigation. If it still leads with open-ended browsing language, compare it against Apify's **"Find your Actor"**. One invites exploration. The other assumes the visitor already has a job to do. If your visitors arrive informed, your nav should reflect that.
