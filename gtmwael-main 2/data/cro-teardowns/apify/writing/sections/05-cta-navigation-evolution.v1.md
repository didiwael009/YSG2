## How Apify's navigation shift from 'Free store' to 'MCP Give your AI access to Actors' redefined its sales motion

### How Apify's CTA changes split one funnel into two

**Evidence check before writing:**
CTAs added: **"Read the announcement"**, **"Contact sales"**, **"Read more customer stories"**, **"Get started"**, **"Get a demo"**, **"New MCP connectors are live"**, **"Find your Actor"**, **"Browse Apify Store"**, **"Open-source tools"**, **"Cloud deployment"**
CTA removed: **"Browse all actors"**

The original **"Browse all actors"** assumed a visitor who was still exploring — someone who needed to see the product catalogue before committing to anything. The new CTAs assume no such hesitation.

**"Get started"** points at a developer ready to build. **"Contact sales"** and **"Get a demo"** point at a buyer who answers to a budget committee. **"Read more customer stories"** points at someone who needs internal sign-off before moving forward.

Three different visitors. Three different next steps. All served from the same homepage — which suggests Apify is now running two parallel funnels side by side rather than funneling everyone toward a single self-serve entry point.

---

### What Apify's navigation additions signal about its new target buyer

The nav additions most worth noting are **"New MCP connectors are live"** and the framing around MCP as a live product announcement. MCP — Model Context Protocol — is a technical integration layer that lets AI agents call external tools. Only someone actively building AI agents would recognize that term on sight.

Placing it in the navigation likely filters out casual visitors without a word of explanation. That may be exactly the point.

**"Find your Actor"** and **"Browse Apify Store"** replace the old browse-first logic with something more directed — less "look around," more "find the specific thing you need."

**Founder test:** Open your own nav. If the first link a visitor sees is **"Pricing"** or a free-trial entry point, your navigation is optimized for price-first conversion. If that is not the buyer you want to attract, your nav is doing the wrong job.
