# How Foreplay rewrote its homepage over 5 years

In January 2021, Foreplay's homepage was a black screen with eight words on it: **"We practice dope design & sell sh\*t online."** No product. No pricing. No screenshots. The navigation offered **Work**, **Services**, and **Hire Us**, and the only call to action was a contact form headlined **"LET'S TANGO."** It was, by every visible signal, a creative agency's site.

By 2026 the same domain sells a software subscription. The headline reads **"The Complete Winning Ad Workflow,"** the page is full of product screenshots and customer logos, and **Hire Us** is gone — replaced by **Start Free Trial**. This teardown walks the five-year path between those two pages, using only what the archived homepages actually show, and explains the business reason behind the change. By the end you'll know whether the same move is available to your own product — and the one condition under which copying it would cost you.

## Quick answer

Foreplay's biggest homepage change was replacing its agency headline **"We practice dope design & sell sh\*t online"** with the product headline **"The Complete Winning Ad Workflow,"** and removing **Hire Us** in favor of **Start Free Trial**. The page stopped selling a service and started selling software. If your services revenue still funds your business, making that swap before your product can carry the load can strand the buyers who pay you today.

## The visual arc: from black-screen statement to enterprise workflow

The homepage didn't flip overnight. Across nine archived snapshots it moves through three clearly different design eras.

### 2021 — a personality, not a product

The first snapshot is a centered white headline on a pure-black background. The tone is a designer's portfolio: bold, irreverent, built to be remembered rather than to explain anything. The footer lists a Toronto address and a personal email (zach@foreplay.co), and the only conversion path is a **"LET'S TANGO"** contact form ending in a **SHOOT** button. Nothing on the page names a software product. A visitor would correctly read this as "hire these people to make ads," not "buy this tool."

### Mid-2022 — the software inflection

By July 2022 the page is unrecognizable. The single black statement is replaced by product screenshots, colored feature tiles, a **"200,000+"** usage stat, customer testimonials, and a "Popular Questions" FAQ. This is the largest single jump in the dataset — not a refresh but a genre change, from agency statement to product landing page. The page now has something to demonstrate, and it spends its space demonstrating it.

### 2024–2026 — enterprise polish

The later snapshots tighten the same product story into an enterprise-grade page. The background flips from black to light, customer logos sit near the top, and product screenshots are integrated directly into the hero rather than shown as distant tiles. By January 2026 the meta description has become **"From advertising inspiration to creative analytics"** — a platform claim, not a service pitch.

**Test this on your own site:** read only your headline and your top nav labels. Could a first-time visitor tell whether you sell a service or a product? If the answer is unclear, your page may still be in statement mode.

## What the words changed to

Three pieces of page copy carry the whole repositioning.

The **headline** moved from **"We practice dope design & sell sh\*t online"** to **"The Complete Winning Ad Workflow."** The first sentence describes the team's attitude; the second names a job the software does. That is the entire agency-to-product shift compressed into one line.

The **page title** — the text that shows in a Google result — moved from **"Foreplay | D2C Creative Advertising Agency"** to **"Foreplay | The Complete Winning Ad Workflow - Save ads from Ad Libraries."** Notice the appended subtitle. It names a concrete task — *save ads from ad libraries* — that someone would actually type into a search bar. The title is now doing keyword work the original never attempted.

The **meta description** shifted from **"Foreplay is a D2C creative advertising agency. We help brands grow through products, services and software"** to a description of what the tool does: save ads from TikTok and the Facebook Ad Library, organize them, and share with a team. The earlier version hedged across "products, services and software." The later one commits to one thing.

## What happened to the buttons and the nav

Page copy tells you the intended story; the calls to action tell you the business model. Foreplay's changed completely.

**Removed:** **Hire Us**, **Our Work**, **More Work**, **View Project**, **Continue to Checkout**, and named client showcases like **Strike**, **OwnersBox**, and **Healthy Crunch**. Every one of these is an agency or portfolio signal — proof that *these specific humans did this specific work for these specific brands.*

**Added:** **Start Free Trial**, **Get Free Trial**, **Start For Free**, **Download App**, **Save & Organize**, **24/7 Ad Library Scraper**, and **Easily Share & Collaborate.** Every one is a self-serve software signal — a visitor activating an account on their own, with no human in the loop.

This is the clearest evidence in the whole teardown. The page didn't just change how it talks; it changed who does the work after the click. A named-client portfolio was replaced by a trial funnel — two different businesses on the same URL.

## Why the homepage changed

The page changes trace back to a real change in what Foreplay sells, and the founder's history explains it.

Foreplay's founder, Zachary Murray, previously ran a D2C home-decor brand called Nomadic Fabrics. According to public interviews, his team's weekly habit of digging competitor ads out of the Facebook Ad Library was painful and disorganized — and after selling that brand, he started Foreplay to fix it. Foreplay reportedly began life as a creative *agency* (which is exactly what the 2021 homepage shows) and then evolved into a self-serve software product. The homepage you see shifting from "Hire Us" to "Start Free Trial" is that business model change happening in public.

What's notable is *how* it grew. Public sources indicate Foreplay is bootstrapped — no venture funding — and has still reached a reported $1.2M in annual revenue with roughly 11 people, serving 30,000+ users including brands like Lululemon and Paramount. The product line expanded from a simple ad-saving Swipe File to Discovery (a community-curated library of 200M+ ads) and Spyder (competitor ad tracking). The homepage's growing feature density mirrors that expanding product surface, one snapshot at a time.

The category context matters too. Between 2021 and 2025, ad-creative and "swipe file" tooling became its own SaaS niche, pulled forward by the volume of performance creative brands now run on Meta and TikTok. Foreplay's move from agency personality toward **"The Complete Winning Ad Workflow"** is consistent with claiming a category position as that niche formed.

## What SaaS teams can borrow — and what to avoid

### Change your nav labels when you change your business, not before

Foreplay's strongest signal wasn't its headline — it was deleting **Hire Us** and adding **Start Free Trial.** Nav labels are the fastest way a visitor reads your business model. If your product is ready to carry revenue, update them decisively the way Foreplay did. The risk runs the other way too: leaving "Hire Us" up while you're really selling software sends mixed signals that cost you both kinds of buyer.

### Put the searchable job in your page title, not just your hero

Foreplay never had to rewrite its whole hero to start ranking. It appended **"Save ads from Ad Libraries"** to its page title — a specific task people search for — while the brand headline stayed aspirational. You can do the same: keep your positioning line in the hero, and let the title tag name the concrete job your best users hired you to do.

### Don't remove your proof before the replacement is validated

Here's the caution. Foreplay removed named-client proof — Strike, OwnersBox, Healthy Crunch — at roughly the same time it removed the agency CTAs. For a bootstrapped company that may have been the right call, but it's the riskiest part of the move to copy. If your services revenue still exceeds your product revenue, pulling down the proof that drives your highest-converting inbound channel before your trial funnel is proven can leave you with neither. Run the new path alongside the old one first; dismantle the old architecture only once the trial is clearly outperforming it.
