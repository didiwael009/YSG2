## How Foreplay's messaging shifted from 'Dope Design' to 'Complete Winning Ad Workflow' — and what that signals

Beneath that visual overhaul, the words themselves changed just as sharply.

### Foreplay's headline shift: from agency voice to category claim

The old headline was **"We practice dope design & Sell Sh*t Online"** — a first-person statement of craft and personality. The new headline is **"The Complete Winning Ad Workflow"** — no "we", no personality, no services listed.

That shift is consistent with a strategy of positioning Foreplay as the default workflow tool for ad teams rather than a hireable service provider. Category ownership means buyers think of your product first when a specific job arises — not just one feature among many.

### How Foreplay's meta description and page title signals a different kind of searcher

The old meta description read: **"Foreplay is a D2C creative advertising agency. We help brands grow through products, services and software."** The new version reads: **"From advertising inspiration to creative analytics. The best way to save ads from TikTok, and Facebook Ad Library, organize and share with your team."**

The old version names three broad offerings. The new version names two specific platforms and two concrete actions. This is consistent with targeting someone who already has a specific workflow problem — not a buyer evaluating agency partners.

The page title shifted from **"Foreplay | D2C Creative Advertising Agency"** to **"Foreplay | The Complete Winning Ad Workflow - Save ads from Ad Libraries"**. The word "Agency" is gone. A specific job replaced it.

### What Foreplay's messaging shift suggests about who the page is now written for

The old page spoke to brand founders deciding whether to hire an agency. The new page suggests Foreplay is now targeting in-house performance marketers and creative teams who arrive with an active, specific problem — not buyers still forming a view of whether they need help at all.

Eight section headings were added where none existed before. Headings like **"Identify winning patterns and replicate success"** and **"Bringing performance & creative teams together"** name two distinct roles: the performance marketer and the creative strategist. That structure suggests the page is now built to speak to each of those roles in turn.

### Test this on your own homepage right now

Look at your own headline and meta description. Count how many times the word "we" appears. If it leads — "We help brands…", "We practice…" — your page may still be selling your identity rather than the outcome your buyer is searching for.
