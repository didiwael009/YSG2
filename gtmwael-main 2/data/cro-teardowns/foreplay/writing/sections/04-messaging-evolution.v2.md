## How Foreplay's H1 went from 'Dope Design' to 'Complete Winning Ad Workflow' — and what that erased

### Foreplay's headline shift: from agency voice to category claim

The old headline was **"We practice dope design & Sell Sh*t Online"** — a first-person statement of craft and personality. The new headline is **"The Complete Winning Ad Workflow"** — no "we", no personality, no services. If the rest of the page delivers on that framing, this positions Foreplay as the default workflow tool rather than a service provider: the category — meaning they want every ad team to think of Foreplay first when they need a workflow tool, not just one feature.

### How Foreplay's meta description signals a different kind of searcher

The old meta description read: **"Foreplay is a D2C creative advertising agency. We help brands grow through products, services and software."** The new version reads: **"From advertising inspiration to creative analytics. The best way to save ads from TikTok, and Facebook Ad Library, organize and share with your team."**

The old version names three broad offerings. The new version names two specific platforms and two concrete actions. This is consistent with targeting someone who already has a specific workflow problem — not a buyer evaluating agency partners.

### Foreplay's new page structure targets multiple buyer roles at once

The page now appears built for multiple in-house roles simultaneously — not a single decision-maker. The old page title was **"Foreplay | D2C Creative Advertising Agency"**. The new title is **"Foreplay | The Complete Winning Ad Workflow - Save ads from Ad Libraries"**. The word "Agency" is gone. A specific job — saving ads from libraries — replaced it.

Eight section headings were added where none existed before. Headings like **"Identify winning patterns and replicate success"** and **"Bringing performance & creative teams together"** name two distinct roles: the performance marketer and the creative strategist.

That structure suggests the page is now designed to speak to each of those roles in turn — which is consistent with a multi-seat SaaS product, not a services pitch to one buyer.

The practical test: look at the before and after copy you just read. The old headline and meta both start with "We." The new versions contain zero first-person language. Count how many times "we" appears in your own headline and meta description. If it leads, your page may still be selling your identity — not the outcome your buyer is searching for.
