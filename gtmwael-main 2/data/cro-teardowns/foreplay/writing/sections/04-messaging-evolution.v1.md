## How Foreplay's H1 went from 'Dope Design' to 'Complete Winning Ad Workflow' — and what that erased

### Foreplay's headline shift: from agency voice to category claim

The old headline was **"We practice dope design & Sell Sh*t Online"** — a first-person statement of craft and personality. The new headline is **"The Complete Winning Ad Workflow"** — no "we", no personality, no services. That shift suggests Foreplay stopped selling a relationship and started claiming to own a category: the end-to-end process of finding, analyzing, and replicating winning ads.

### How the meta description signals a different kind of searcher

The old meta description read: **"Foreplay is a D2C creative advertising agency. We help brands grow through products, services and software."** The new version reads: **"From advertising inspiration to creative analytics. The best way to save ads from TikTok, and Facebook Ad Library, organize and share with your team."**

The old version names three broad offerings. The new version names two specific platforms — TikTok and Facebook Ad Library — and two concrete actions. That is consistent with targeting someone who already has a specific workflow problem, not a brand-growth buyer evaluating agency partners.

### What the messaging shift signals about who the page is now written for

The page title change reinforces this. The old title was **"Foreplay | D2C Creative Advertising Agency"**. The new title is **"Foreplay | The Complete Winning Ad Workflow - Save ads from Ad Libraries"**. The word "Agency" is gone. A specific job — saving ads from libraries — replaced it.

Eight section headings were added where none existed before. Headings like **"Identify winning patterns and replicate success"** and **"Bringing performance & creative teams together"** suggest the page is now structured to speak to multiple roles at once: the performance marketer and the creative strategist. That is a homepage built for a multi-seat SaaS product, not a services pitch to a single decision-maker.

The practical test: visit your own homepage and count how many times the word "we" appears versus how many times a specific buyer outcome or tool context appears. If "we" wins, your page is still selling your identity — not the result the buyer is searching for.
