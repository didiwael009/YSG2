## How Foreplay replaced 'Hire Us' and 'View Project' with a four-product nav and a free trial funnel

### Foreplay's CTA changes: from client transactions to free trial signup

The original CTAs assumed a visitor ready to hire someone. **"Hire Us"**, **"Continue to Checkout"**, **"Our Work"**, **"More Work"**, and named client campaigns — **"Strike Launching a Pre-Workout"**, **"OwnersBox Drew Brees' Fantasy Hotline"**, **"Healthy Crunch Sling'n Snacks"** — all pointed toward a services transaction.

The new CTAs assume a visitor ready to try something themselves, without talking to anyone at Foreplay first. **"Start Free Trial"**, **"Get Free Trial"**, and **"Start For Free"** replace every agency-era action. That is a complete reversal of what the page expects the visitor to do next.

Named client campaigns provided proof — real brands, real results. Removing them before a replacement trust signal was in place leaves cold visitors with no evidence of real-world outcomes. If those campaigns were the primary reason a skeptical visitor converted, their absence creates a gap that free-trial messaging alone may not fill until social proof or usage data replaces them.

Four added CTAs describe specific jobs the product performs: **"Save & Organize"**, **"Automate Transcription"**, **"Easily Share & Collaborate"**, and **"24/7 Ad Library Scraper"**. Each one targets a different task. This suggests the page is now addressing more than one type of user rather than a single buyer weighing whether to hire an agency.

### Foreplay's navigation changes: what the page stopped needing to explain

The evidence records four navigation items removed and eight added. The removed items are not individually named in the data — but every CTA that disappeared carried agency-era language. The pattern suggests the navigation was cleared of anything that implied a client relationship.

What arrived instead points toward a product with distinct tools. Eight navigation items were added, signaling the company now needs to orient visitors across multiple product areas rather than explain a single service.

**Founder test:** Foreplay removed four nav items — likely labels tied to agency work — and replaced them with job-specific CTAs like **"Save & Organize"** and **"Automate Transcription"**. Count how many of your own nav items describe what you do *for* a client versus what a visitor can do *themselves* right now. Any label that implies a handoff is agency framing in disguise.
