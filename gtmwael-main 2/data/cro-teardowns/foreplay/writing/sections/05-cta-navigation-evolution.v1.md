## How Foreplay replaced 'Hire Us' and 'View Project' with a four-product nav and a free trial funnel

### CTA changes: from "hire us" to "start free trial"

The original CTAs assumed a visitor ready to pay someone. **"Hire Us"**, **"Continue to Checkout"**, and named client campaigns — **"Strike Launching a Pre-Workout"**, **"OwnersBox Drew Brees' Fantasy Hotline"**, **"Healthy Crunch Sling'n Snacks"** — all pointed toward a services transaction.

The new CTAs assume a visitor ready to try something themselves. **"Start Free Trial"**, **"Get Free Trial"**, and **"Start For Free"** all point to self-serve product entry. That is a complete reversal of what the page expects the visitor to do next.

The specific CRO risk: those named client projects were proof. Real brands, real campaigns. Removing them before free trial conversion data could validate the replacement means the page may now convert at a lower rate — particularly for visitors arriving cold with no prior knowledge of the product.

---

### Navigation changes: what Foreplay stopped needing to explain

Four items disappeared from the navigation: **"Work"**, **"Services"**, **"Hire Us"**, and one additional removed item the evidence records but does not name. Their removal signals the company no longer wants visitors thinking about an agency relationship.

What replaced them points toward a product with distinct tools. Added CTAs like **"Save & Organize"**, **"Automate Transcription"**, **"Easily Share & Collaborate"**, and **"24/7 Ad Library Scraper"** each describe a specific job the product does — suggesting multiple buyer roles are being addressed simultaneously rather than one purchase decision.

**Founder test:** Pull up your own nav. If any item requires a conversation before the visitor understands what it means, it may be doing agency work — not product work.
