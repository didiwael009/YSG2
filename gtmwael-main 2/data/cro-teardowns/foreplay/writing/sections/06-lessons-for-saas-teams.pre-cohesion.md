## What SaaS teams can study from Foreplay's decision to kill its agency before the SaaS was ready

### Run a parallel CTA test before removing the services path — what Foreplay skipped

Foreplay removed **"Hire Us"** from navigation and replaced **"View Project · Continue to Checkout"** with **"Start Free Trial · Start For Free"** in a single sweep. Named client proof — Strike, OwnersBox, Healthy Crunch — disappeared at the same time. That is two conversion paths removed at once, with no reported test validating the replacement.

This matters if your services revenue still exceeds your SaaS revenue. Removing agency social proof before a trial funnel is validated may eliminate your highest-converting inbound channel before the replacement can carry the load.

Visit your homepage and add a **"Try Free"** CTA next to your existing primary CTA — without removing anything else. Run it for 30 days. If the trial path generates more qualified signups than the services path, then dismantle the old architecture.

### Category claims must match feature depth — where Foreplay's navigation ran ahead

Foreplay's new navigation lists **"LensAdvertising analytics for creative teams"** as a named product alongside Swipe File and Discovery. Naming a feature in the nav sets a visitor expectation. If Lens launched after that nav item appeared, any visitor who clicked found an underdeveloped product — and that gap is harder to recover from than modest positioning.

As Dunford describes in *Obviously Awesome*, positioning that outruns the product creates a trust problem that features alone cannot fix. Visitors who arrive expecting parity leave with a negative impression that sticks.

Open your own navigation and list every named product or feature. For each one, ask: could a first-time visitor complete a meaningful workflow today? If the answer is no for any item, remove or relabel it until the depth is there.

### Use the SEO page title as an audience signal — what Foreplay's subtitle pivot shows

Foreplay added **"Save ads from Ad Libraries"** to its page title — shifting from **"Foreplay | D2C Creative Advertising Agency"** to **"Foreplay | The Complete Winning Ad Workflow - Save ads from Ad Libraries"**. That subtitle names a specific job — saving ads — without touching the hero headline. It targets a searcher who already knows the task they want to complete.

This approach lets you pivot which audience finds you through search without rewriting your homepage. A specific job-to-be-done in the title attracts tool-seekers at the top of the funnel, before the full platform is built.

Open your current page title in a browser tab. Check whether it names a category label or a concrete task. If it says "platform," "solution," or your company name only, test appending one specific job your best users hired you for.
