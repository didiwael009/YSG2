## Vercel homepage messaging: from developer tool to AI platform category

### Who the old page served

The 2021 homepage spoke to frontend developers comparing deployment tools. The headline **"Develop. Preview. Ship."** described three workflow stages — what happens when you use the product. The meta description promised **"the best frontend developer experience"** — a pitch built on how the tool feels to use, not what business problem it solves.

### Who the new page serves

The 2026 headline **"Build and deploy on the AI Cloud"** names a platform category without explaining it. A visitor unfamiliar with the term may need to scroll to understand what Vercel does.

The meta description now lists **"frameworks, workflows, and infrastructure"** — three platform components instead of one developer benefit. This language is consistent with a buyer comparing complete platforms rather than evaluating a single-purpose tool.

### What this means for the sales process

Open your homepage in an incognito window. Can a visitor who has never heard of your category understand what you do from the headline alone? If not, you are filtering for buyers who already recognize the platform type they need — intentionally or not.

The shift from workflow description to category claim suggests Vercel expects visitors to arrive with context. The headline no longer teaches what the product does. It identifies which category the product belongs to, which works when the buyer already knows they need an AI-enabled deployment platform.
