## Who Vercel's homepage messaging now targets

### Who the old page served

The original page spoke to developers already familiar with frontend deployment workflows. The headline **"Develop. Preview. Ship."** assumed the reader knew what those three stages meant and why they mattered. The meta description promised **"the best frontend developer experience"** — language for someone evaluating tools within a known category.

### Who the new page serves

The new page addresses decision-makers comparing infrastructure platforms, not just developers choosing a deployment tool. **"Build and deploy on the AI Cloud"** positions Vercel as infrastructure, not workflow software. The meta description now mentions **"frameworks, workflows, and infrastructure"** — three capability areas instead of one outcome. This is language for buyers who need to justify a platform purchase across multiple use cases.

### What this means for the sales process

The shift suggests Vercel now expects visitors who will involve procurement, compare vendors, and evaluate contracts — not just developers signing up to deploy a side project. The page sets up a sales conversation, not a self-serve trial. Does your homepage still speak to the person who will use your product daily, or to the person who will approve the purchase?
