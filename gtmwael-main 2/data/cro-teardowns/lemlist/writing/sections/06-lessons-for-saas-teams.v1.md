## Should SaaS companies copy lemlist's homepage strategy? When it works and when it doesn't

### The pattern

This is feature-set expansion disguised as repositioning. lemlist built additional products around its cold email core — lead database, phone finder, multichannel sequences — then rewrote the homepage to name the entire stack instead of the original workflow. The shift from **"Send cold emails that get replies"** to **"The AI Outbound Platform"** only works because the product truly became a platform.

### Who should copy this

If three or more of your existing customers use at least three of your features together in a single workflow, you have earned platform positioning. If your customer success team already talks about your product as a "stack" or "system" instead of a tool, the homepage can follow. If enterprise buyers ask "what else does this integrate with?" before they ask "how does the core feature work?", you are ready.

### Who should NOT copy this

If most of your signups still come from searches for your original use case — the single problem you first solved — do not remove that language from your H1. Platform positioning before platform usage kills the conversion path that built your business. You will stop new trials without replacing them with qualified demos, because buyers searching for a specific solution will not recognize your page as the answer.

### The test before you copy

Open your product analytics. Filter for customers acquired in the last 90 days. What percentage activated three or more features in their first month? If that number is below 30%, your homepage still needs to speak to single-use-case buyers. Keep the outcome-focused headline.
