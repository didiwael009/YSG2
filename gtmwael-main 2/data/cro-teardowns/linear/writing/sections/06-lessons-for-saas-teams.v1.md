## Should SaaS companies copy Linear's homepage strategy? When it works and when it doesn't

### The pattern

Linear stopped selling against the existing category and started owning a category that doesn't fully exist yet. The page assumes AI agents are already part of the product development workflow — treating it as infrastructure to buy, not a future to prepare for. This is category creation through forward positioning: claiming the category language before competitors arrive.

### Who should copy this

Your product is already being used to manage outputs from AI coding tools like Cursor or GitHub Copilot in production environments. You have at least three enterprise customers who will publicly say they use your tool to coordinate human-AI workflows. Your support tickets and sales calls include "AI agent" as buyer language, not just marketing speculation. You have evidence that this workflow exists today in your user base.

### Who should NOT copy this

Your differentiation is speed, ease of use, or price compared to an established tool. "AI agent" appears nowhere in your customer research, demos, or support conversations. If you claim to be purpose-built for AI workflows that your buyers are not practicing yet, you will confuse the buyer who came to replace Jira and lose credibility with the buyer who actually manages AI agents in production.

### The test before you copy

Search your support tickets and sales call transcripts for "AI agent" or the names of AI coding tools. If those terms appear in fewer than 10% of conversations, your buyers are not yet living in the workflow this positioning describes. Keep your functional category anchor and test AI messaging on a separate landing page first.
