## How a $140M Series B, the Intellimize acquisition, and an 8% layoff forced Webflow to pick a buyer

Webflow reportedly reached a $2.1 billion valuation after its 2021 Series B, then a reported $4 billion after its Series C — with enterprise revenue still a fraction of total ARR. At that scale, investors expect enterprise contracts to carry growth. The no-code market was also fragmenting fast, with Framer taking the designer segment and AI-native site builders threatening the self-serve base from below.

The homepage shifts are consistent with that pressure. The title changed from **"Responsive web design tool, CMS, and hosting platform"** to **"Webflow: The agentic web platform for modern businesses"** — dropping feature description in favor of a category claim. Webflow's 2024 acquisition of Intellimize, reportedly framed as a move toward becoming a "Website Experience Platform," points to a deliberate expansion toward marketing leaders rather than individual designers.

The 2024 layoffs, which reportedly redirected resources toward AI-first features, suggest the same pattern playing out in product. Other SaaS companies — HubSpot, Contentful, Optimizely — made near-identical upmarket moves when self-serve growth plateaued. For SaaS teams watching this: a homepage can only serve one buyer clearly. Webflow appears to have chosen.
