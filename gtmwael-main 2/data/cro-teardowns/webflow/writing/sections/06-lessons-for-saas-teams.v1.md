## What SaaS teams can study from Webflow's decision to stop writing for the community that built it

### Secondary CTA demotion — Webflow moved "Start for free" out of the primary position without removing it

Webflow's old primary CTA was **"Get started — it's free"**. The new page leads with **"Contact Sales"** and keeps **"Start for free"** as a secondary option. The free tier did not disappear — it was demoted. That is a meaningful distinction.

This pattern is worth testing if your self-serve conversion rate is healthy but your average deal size has stalled. Demoting the free CTA signals that the page is now optimized for enterprise pipeline, not trial volume — and that only works if your sales team can actually handle the inbound.

Visit your homepage right now. Count which CTA appears first. Then ask: does your sales team have a response SLA and a qualification playbook ready for those leads? If not, promoting **"Contact Sales"** may generate a queue your team cannot convert.

---

### Coining a category before the market names it — Webflow launched "Explore AEO" as a standalone CTA

Webflow added **"Explore AEO"** as its own CTA — for a term most of its visitors had likely never searched before. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization — getting your content surfaced in AI-generated answers, not just traditional search results) is a phrase Webflow appears to be actively defining, not just using.

This is worth attempting if you have enough brand authority that visitors trust you to teach them something new. If visitors still arrive unsure what your product does, adding a CTA for a coined term will create confusion, not curiosity.

Open your homepage and read your CTAs aloud to someone unfamiliar with your product. If they ask "what does that mean?" — you do not yet have the brand recognition to coin a category. Wait until your core offer is understood by default.

---

### Persona narrowing audit — Webflow removed "Who uses Webflow" and "Free until you're ready to launch"

Both removed headings served a specific visitor: someone exploring whether Webflow was for people like them, or worried about cost before committing. Replacing them with **"300,000+ brands move the needle with Webflow"** and **"Make your website your competitive edge"** signals that Webflow expects its new target buyer to arrive already qualified.

This matters because legacy copy written for an earlier audience can actively filter out the buyer you are trying to reach now. A founder reassured by "free until you're ready" is a different person from a CMO who needs peer validation at scale.

Go to your homepage and highlight every line that reduces personal risk — phrases like "no credit card required" or "free forever." Then ask: is that line speaking to your current target buyer, or to the audience that found you three years ago?
