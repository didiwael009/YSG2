## Three homepage moves from Webflow's evolution that SaaS teams can test

### Secondary CTA demotion — Webflow moved "Start for free" out of the primary position without removing it

Webflow's old primary CTA was **"Get started — it's free"**. The new page leads with **"Contact Sales"** and keeps **"Start for free"** as a secondary option. The free tier did not disappear — it was moved down. That is a meaningful distinction.

This pattern is worth testing if your self-serve conversion rate is healthy but your average deal size has stalled. The demotion suggests the page may now be prioritised for enterprise pipeline (inbound leads from larger companies with a sales process) over trial volume — and that only works if your sales team can handle the inbound.

Visit your homepage right now. Count which CTA appears first. Then ask: does your sales team have a response time target and a qualification playbook ready for those leads? If not, promoting **"Contact Sales"** may generate a queue your team cannot convert.

---

### Coining a category before the market names it — Webflow launched "Explore AEO" as a standalone CTA

Webflow added **"Explore AEO"** as its own CTA — for a term most visitors had likely never searched before. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization — getting your content surfaced in AI-generated answers, not just traditional search results) is a phrase Webflow appears to be actively defining, not just using.

This is worth attempting if you have enough brand recognition that visitors trust you to teach them something new. If visitors still arrive unsure what your product does, adding a CTA for a coined term may create confusion rather than curiosity — especially if your core offer is not yet understood by default.

Open your homepage and read your CTAs aloud to someone unfamiliar with your product. If they ask "what does that mean?" — your brand may not yet have the recognition to coin a category. Consider waiting until your core offer lands without explanation.

---

### Persona narrowing — Webflow removed "Who uses Webflow" and "Free until you're ready to launch"

Persona narrowing — tightening who the page speaks to — is visible in two removed headings. Both served a visitor exploring whether Webflow was right for them or worried about cost. Replacing them with **"300,000+ brands move the needle with Webflow"** and **"Make your website your competitive edge"** signals an expectation that new visitors arrive already informed.

This matters because copy written for an earlier audience can actively filter out the buyer you are trying to reach now. A founder reassured by "free until you're ready" is a different person from a CMO who needs peer validation at scale.

Go to your homepage and highlight every line that reduces personal risk — phrases like "no credit card required" or "free forever." Then ask: is that line speaking to your current target buyer, or to the audience that found you three years ago?
